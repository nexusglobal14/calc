About the Calorie & TDEE Calculator

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is an estimate of the calories you burn in a day, including exercise. This calculator first estimates your basal metabolic rate (BMR), the energy your body uses at rest, and then multiplies it by an activity factor. It also shows a calorie level that is 500 kcal below maintenance.

How to use it

  1. Select your sex and enter your age.
  2. Enter your weight in kilograms and height in centimeters.
  3. Choose the activity level that best matches your week.
  4. Read your BMR, maintenance calories (TDEE) and weight loss target.

The formula

The BMR uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5 for men, and − 161 instead of + 5 for women. TDEE is BMR multiplied by an activity factor: 1.2 sedentary, 1.375 light, 1.55 moderate, 1.725 heavy. The weight loss target is TDEE minus 500 kcal.

Worked example

A 25-year-old man who is 70 kg and 175 cm tall has a BMR of 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 175 − 5 × 25 + 5 = 1,674 kcal. At a moderate activity level (1.55), TDEE is about 2,594 kcal and the weight loss target is about 2,094 kcal.

Things to keep in mind

These numbers are estimates and individual metabolism varies. A 500 kcal daily deficit is a commonly cited rule of thumb for about 0.5 kg of weight change per week, but real results vary. Very low calorie intakes are not suitable for everyone. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding women and people with medical conditions should get personal advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the energy you use at rest. TDEE adds the energy used by daily activity and exercise.

Which activity level should I choose?

Pick the level that matches your typical week, and choose the lower one if you are unsure, since people tend to overestimate activity.

Why Mifflin-St Jeor?

It is widely used and generally considered more accurate than older equations for most adults.

Is a 500 kcal deficit right for me?

It may not be. Speak to a doctor or registered dietitian before making major changes to your diet.
