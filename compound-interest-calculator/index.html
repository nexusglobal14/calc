About the Compound Interest Calculator

Compound interest means you earn interest on your original deposit and on the interest already added. This calculator shows the future balance of a one-time deposit. Enter the initial amount, the annual interest rate, the number of years and how often interest is compounded.

How to use it

  1. Enter your initial deposit.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate as a percentage.
  3. Enter the number of years.
  4. Choose the compounding frequency: monthly, quarterly or annually.
  5. Read the interest earned and the total future balance.

The formula

The balance is A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × t, where P is the initial deposit, r is the annual rate as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year and t is the number of years. Interest earned is A minus P.

Worked example

A $10,000 deposit at 8% for 5 years grows to about $14,693 with annual compounding. With monthly compounding the balance is about $14,898, roughly $205 more, because interest is added to the balance more often.

Things to keep in mind

This calculator handles a single starting deposit only. It does not include recurring contributions, withdrawals, taxes or fees. For regular monthly contributions, use the SIP calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original amount. Compound interest is calculated on the original amount plus interest already earned.

Does more frequent compounding help?

Yes, slightly. Monthly compounding gives a higher balance than annual compounding at the same rate, but the gap is much smaller than the effect of the rate or the time period.

Can I add monthly contributions?

Not in this tool. Use the SIP calculator for regular monthly investing.

Is the rate the same as APY?

No. You enter the nominal annual rate, and the compounding setting determines the effective yield.
