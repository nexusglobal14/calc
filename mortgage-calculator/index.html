About the Mortgage Loan Calculator

This mortgage calculator estimates the monthly payment (EMI) on a fixed-rate home loan. Enter the home price, your down payment, the annual interest rate and the loan term in years. The tool subtracts the down payment to find the amount borrowed, then shows your monthly payment, the total interest you would pay over the life of the loan and the total amount repaid.

How to use it

  1. Enter the home price in dollars.
  2. Enter your down payment. The loan amount is the home price minus this figure.
  3. Enter the annual interest rate, for example 6.5.
  4. Enter the loan term in years. Common terms are 15 and 30 years.
  5. Read the monthly payment, total interest and total amount paid. Results update as you type.

The formula

The monthly payment uses the standard amortization formula: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments (years × 12). Total paid is EMI × n, and total interest is total paid minus P.

Worked example

A $300,000 home with a $60,000 down payment means a $240,000 loan. At 6.5% interest over 30 years, the monthly payment is about $1,517. Over 360 payments you would repay about $546,107, which means roughly $306,107 of that is interest.

Things to keep in mind

The result covers principal and interest only. Property tax, home insurance, HOA fees and mortgage insurance are not included, so your actual monthly housing cost will be higher. The calculator assumes a fixed rate for the whole term and requires an interest rate above zero.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in the monthly payment?

Only principal and interest. Taxes, insurance and other fees are not included.

How does a bigger down payment change the result?

A larger down payment reduces the amount borrowed, which lowers both the monthly payment and the total interest.

Why is so much interest paid on a 30-year loan?

Interest is charged on the remaining balance each month, and a long term keeps that balance high for many years. A shorter term costs more per month but much less in total interest.

Does this work for adjustable-rate loans?

No. It assumes one fixed rate for the full term.
