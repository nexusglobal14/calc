About the Pregnancy Due Date Calculator

This calculator estimates your estimated due date (EDD) from the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). Select that date and the tool adds 280 days, which is 40 weeks, using the method known as Naegele's rule.

How to use it

  1. Select the first day of your last menstrual period.
  2. Read your estimated due date.

The formula

Due date = first day of last period + 280 days (40 weeks). This is equivalent to adding one year, subtracting three months and adding seven days.

Worked example

If the first day of your last period was January 1, 2026, adding 280 days gives an estimated due date of October 8, 2026.

Things to keep in mind

Naegele's rule assumes a regular 28-day cycle with ovulation around day 14. If your cycles are shorter, longer or irregular, or you conceived through IVF, the true date can differ. Only about 4 to 5 percent of babies are born on the exact due date, and a normal full-term birth can happen from roughly 37 to 42 weeks. Your provider may adjust the date after an early ultrasound.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is a due date?

It is an estimate. Most babies are born within a couple of weeks either side of it.

What if my cycle is not 28 days?

The calculation may be off by several days. Ask your provider to confirm the date.

How many weeks is a full-term pregnancy?

Full term is considered 39 to 40 weeks. Early term is 37 to 38 weeks, and late term is 41 weeks.

What if I do not know my last period date?

Contact a healthcare provider. An ultrasound can estimate gestational age.
